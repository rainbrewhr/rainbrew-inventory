# Writing

Applies to UI copy, docs, emails, marketing, commit messages, and anything else made of words. Words in a product exist to make it easier to understand and use.

## Process
1. State in one sentence what the text says and who it's for. If you can't, the problem is upstream of style.
2. Write with the rules below.
3. Scan for the patterns below and rewrite the sentence; don't swap in a synonym.
4. Delete any sentence that can go without losing information.
5. When editing someone else's text, keep their voice, facts, and meaning.

## Rules
- Lead with substance: the answer, the ask, the claim. No warm-up.
- Be specific: a number, name, example, or mechanism. With no specifics, say less.
- Say it once. No closing paragraph restating the opening.
- Commit. Hedge only for real uncertainty, and name it.
- Plain words: "use" not "utilize", "help" not "facilitate".
- Vary sentence length. Match the register of the context (a toast is not a keynote).
- No flattery, no performed enthusiasm, no announcing what you're about to do.

## UI copy specifics
- Name things the way users do, not how the system is built: users manage notifications, not webhook config.
- Describe what something is or does; don't sell it.
- Buttons are verbs that say what happens. Same action, same name everywhere.
- Errors: what happened, how to fix it, where it happened. No blame, no apology.
- Empty states: an invitation to act, with the action present.
- Confirmations name what they affect ("Delete 3 files").
- Sentence case. Let each element do one job.
- Use the app's voice and label vocabulary from `DESIGN.md`.

## Patterns to remove
- **Filler:** "Great question!", "Certainly!", "I'd be happy to help", "In today's fast-paced world", "In conclusion", "I hope this helps".
- **Stock vocabulary:** delve, tapestry, testament, landscape (figurative), realm, navigate (figurative), leverage, harness, unlock, elevate, foster, robust, seamless, holistic, pivotal, game-changer, cutting-edge, "it's worth noting".
- **Formulas:** "It's not just X, it's Y"; reflexive triples; question-then-instant-answer; colon reveals ("The result? Chaos."); every paragraph ending on a quotable line; em dashes as a tic.
- **Imposed structure:** headers and bullets on what is really a short argument.
- **Fake depth:** unsourced "experts say", sweeping claims of significance, abstract nouns doing a verb's job.
- **Placeholder content:** "John Doe", "Acme", lorem ipsum, "10x faster", round invented stats.

## Example
Slop: "In today's fast-paced digital landscape, it's crucial to leverage robust solutions that seamlessly streamline your workflow."
Fixed: "Syncs your calendar and task list so you stop copying meetings into your to-do app."
